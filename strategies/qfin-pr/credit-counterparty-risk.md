# Credit & Counterparty Risk — learnings from the q-fin.PR corpus
`312` of 2274 corpus papers matched this category (`309` with PDFs); this doc is based on a close reading of `6`.

## 1. What the corpus actually offers

- **Structural first-passage credit with contagion is fully analytic in two dimensions, and firm volatility dominates.** Two firms as correlated geometric Brownian motions with exponential barriers $b_i(t)=K_ie^{-\gamma_i(T-t)}$ give a closed double-Bessel series for the joint survival probability $P(t)$, hence closed-form bond yields and first/second-to-default CDS spreads; dependence enters via $\rho=-\cos\beta$ (arXiv:0710.0753v1). Within that model $\sigma_i$ has "by far the largest impact on CDS spreads", the write-down factor $\omega_1$ inverts the yield curve as it falls, and the barrier slope $\gamma_i\in[0.01,0.08]$ moves a 5-year yield by under one percentage point at fixed correlation (arXiv:0710.0753v1).
- **Correlation moves first- and second-to-default spreads in opposite directions.** Raising $\rho$ lowers first-to-default and raises second-to-default spreads; the paper's *contagion* channel — the survivor defaults when its partner hits the barrier — drives second-to-default up to equality with first-to-default. The authors flag instant cross-default as "clearly not very realistic", and $N>2$ names have no analytic solution (arXiv:0710.0753v1).
- **Reduced-form intensity models are the practical alternative but are path-dependent in the order of defaults.** The $n^{\text{th}}$ survival function obeys a recursive path-integral identity over the lattice of killed/surviving coordinates; applied to a multivariate Poisson default model it prices $n^{\text{th}}$-to-default CDSs semi-analytically and allows *simultaneous* defaults (arXiv:2509.02347v2). In reported runs ($r=0.02$, $T=5$, three underlyings, arbitrary units) 1st/2nd/3rd-to-default spreads are 1822 / 41.84 / 2.30 with symmetric intensities, 42.48 / 38.84 / 0.06 when single-name intensities are tiny, and 42.48 / 4.61 / 0.66 when only cross-intensities are tiny — spread levels depend sharply on which default type dominates (arXiv:2509.02347v2).
- **Bilateral CVA is an option and it is symmetric.** The adjustment is a zero-strike call on the residual net present value in counterparty-default scenarios minus a zero-strike put in investor-default scenarios; the counterparty computes exactly its negative, which unilateral CVA does not deliver. Its sign can flip as relative credit quality moves, and a worsening of one's own credit widens the own-default term — the mechanism behind Citigroup's 2009 "$2.5 USD billion positive CVA on derivative positions" (arXiv:0812.3705v4).
- **Default correlation and credit-spread volatility both move bilateral CVA, and they interact.** With a Gaussian trivariate copula on exponential triggers and independent CIR intensities, raising the reference-credit vol parameter $\nu_1$ from 0.01 to 0.50 lifts CDS-implied vol from 1.5% to 42% and moves a Lehman/British-Airways mark-to-market from 39.1 bp to 63.8 bp in the high-correlation scenario while a receiver-side adjustment barely moves; with low spread volatility and high correlation, wrong-way risk can *decrease* (arXiv:0812.3705v4).
- **Jumps, not diffusion, reproduce CDS stylised facts.** A Merton-type model with downward-only exponential jumps and an exogenous constant barrier, calibrated daily to the full 0.5–30y CDS term structure of 84 European firms (105,756 spread observations, 2017-01-03 to 2021-12-31), prices bonds and CDSs by inverse Laplace transform and shows *no* significant relation between calibration error and a transition-risk proxy — while the same model without jumps cannot reproduce the term-structure dynamics at all; a t-test finds no significant difference in calibration-*error level* between the two, so the gain is in dynamics, not fit (arXiv:2303.12483v1).
- **CVA is model-dependent even for products that were not, and CVA change dominated crisis losses.** Pricing an option on the residual value of a plain-vanilla swap book needs an interest-rate option model, so a portfolio that needed only initial curves now needs volatilities and correlations. Per the Basel III quote reproduced in the FAQ, "roughly two-thirds of losses attributed to counterparty credit risk were due to CVA losses and only about one-third were due to actual defaults"; the same source records seven credit events in the month from September 7 to October 8, 2008 (arXiv:1111.1331v3).
- **Exposure profiles for early-exercise books fall out of pricing.** A regress-later network reinterpreted as a static portfolio of European calls and puts produces Expected Exposure and 99% Potential Future Exposure at exercise dates *and* at intermediate risk horizons, aligning with the COS benchmark better than Longstaff–Schwartz at every horizon tested, converging in 3 epochs versus 25 for the un-optimised network (arXiv:2402.15936v1).
- **The corpus disagrees on copulas and on the structural/intensity choice.** The structural paper calls copulas static, causally empty and time-unstable (arXiv:0710.0753v1); the bilateral-CVA paper adopts a Gaussian copula precisely to separate spread dynamics from default dependence (arXiv:0812.3705v4); the $n^{\text{th}}$-CDS paper notes copulas remain popular for baskets but treat the *sequence* of defaults differently (arXiv:2509.02347v2). Intensity models are "often preferred by practitioners" (arXiv:2509.02347v2) while structural models are "far more grounded in economic fundamentals" (arXiv:0710.0753v1).

## 2. Implementable formulas & algorithms

### 2.1 Two-firm structural first-passage pricing with contagion
**Purpose.** Price corporate zero-coupon bonds and first/second-to-default CDS baskets from firm-value dynamics, with dependence from asset correlation *and* contagion.

$$dV_i=(r_f-q_i)V_i\,dt+\sigma_iV_i\,dW_i,\quad X_i(t)=\alpha_it+\sigma_iW_i(t),\quad \alpha_i=r_f-q_i-\gamma_i-\tfrac12\sigma_i^2,\quad \tau_i=\inf\{t:X_i(t)=B_i\}$$
$$P(t)=\frac{2}{\beta t}e^{a_1B_1+a_2B_2+bt}e^{-r_0^2/2t}\sum_{n\ge1}\sin\!\Big(\frac{n\pi\theta_0}{\beta}\Big)\!\int_0^{\beta}\!\sin\!\Big(\frac{n\pi\theta}{\beta}\Big)g_n(\theta)\,d\theta,\qquad \tan\beta=-\frac{\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}{\rho}$$
$$y_1(0,T)=-\frac1T\ln\frac{C_1(0,T)}{K_1},\qquad c_{\text{first}}=(1-R)\frac{1-e^{-r_fT}P(T)-\int_0^Tr_fe^{-r_fs}P(s)ds}{\int_0^Te^{-r_fs}P(s)ds},\qquad c_{\text{second}}\ \text{uses}\ P^{(2)}(t)=\sum_iP(\tau_i>t)-P(t)$$
- **Inputs:** firm values (or equity as proxy), par $K_i$, $\sigma_i$, $\rho$, $q_i$, the risk-free curve, recovery $R$, write-down $\omega_1$.
- **Parameters (reported):** $\sigma_i=0.2$ yr$^{-1/2}$, $K_1=100$, $r_f=0.05$, $q_i=0$, $\gamma_i=0.03$, $V_i(0)/b_i(0)=2$ (scaled distance to default 3.5), $R=0.5$ (sensitivity $R\in[0.3,0.7]$) (arXiv:0710.0753v1).
- **Observed effects:** yields fall monotonically in $\rho$; lowering $\omega_1$ inverts the yield curve and $\omega_1=1$ makes the bond risk-free; first-to-default falls and second-to-default rises with $\rho$; contagion collapses second-to-default onto first-to-default (arXiv:0710.0753v1).
- **Failure modes:** immediate cross-default at the partner's barrier; only $\rho\ge0$ credible; three-dimensional sparse-grid quadrature required; no analytic extension past two names; $\gamma_i$-insensitivity holds only for $T\le5$y (arXiv:0710.0753v1).

### 2.2 $n^{\text{th}}$-to-default CDS from recursive survival functions
**Purpose.** Price multi-name credit derivatives in an intensity (jump) model where one, several or all names may default simultaneously, without Monte Carlo.

$$S_1(t)=S_2(t)+\int_{\mathbb R}dx\int_0^tds\int_{\mathbb R}dy\,P_1^1(x,t|y,s)P_1^2(y,s|X_2\in\partial R,s)F^2(s)+(\text{symmetric term}),\qquad S_n(t)=S_{n+1}(t)+\!\!\sum_{g\in G_n}\!\!I_g(t)$$
where $I_g$ integrates over time-ordered default sequences weighted by $F^{n+1}(\tau_{n-1})\cdots F^N(\tau_1)$ (arXiv:2509.02347v2, Thms. 1–2). Bivariate Poisson model and its correlation:
$$X_1=Y_1+Y_{12},\quad X_2=Y_2+Y_{12},\qquad \rho=\frac{\lambda_{12}}{\sqrt{(\lambda_1+\lambda_{12})(\lambda_2+\lambda_{12})}},\qquad f=u\sum_{i=1}^{L}e^{-rT_i}S_{N-n+1}(T_i),\quad p=-\int_0^Te^{-rt}F^{N-n+1}(t)dt$$
- **Inputs:** single-name intensities $\lambda_i$, cross-intensities $\lambda_{ij}$ (equivalently $\rho$), payment schedule, constant $r$.
- **Parameters (reported):** $r=0.02$, $T=5$, payments every 0.5; Model A $\lambda_i=\lambda_{ij}=2.5$, Model B $\lambda_i=0.01$, Model C $\lambda_{ij}=0.01$ (arXiv:2509.02347v2).
- **Observed effects:** with time-scale separation between driving processes $S_1(t)$ develops a hump rather than decaying smoothly; spread levels separate sharply by dominant default type (see §1) (arXiv:2509.02347v2).
- **Failure modes:** the path set $G_n$ explodes combinatorially in $N$ (a 2-name toy case is only 3× slower than simulation); after a non-simultaneous default the surviving intensities *shrink*, which the authors concede "is not necessarily the desired behavior"; piecewise-constant intensities only; no credit risk of the CDS writer (arXiv:2509.02347v2).

### 2.3 Bilateral CVA with copula-linked stochastic intensities
**Purpose.** Compute the symmetric counterparty-risk adjustment for a portfolio (here a CDS book) when investor, reference credit and counterparty can all default, with stochastic spreads and correlation between names.

With default-ordering events $A=\{τ_0\leτ_2\le T\},B=\{τ_0\le T\leτ_2\},C=\{τ_2\leτ_0\le T\},D=\{τ_2\le T\leτ_0\}$:
$$\mathbb E_t[\Pi^D]=\mathbb E_t[\Pi]+\mathbb E_t\!\big[LGD_0\mathbf 1_{A\cup B}D(t,τ_0)[-NPV(τ_0)]^+\big]-\mathbb E_t\!\big[LGD_2\mathbf 1_{C\cup D}D(t,τ_2)[NPV(τ_2)]^+\big]$$
i.e. long a zero-strike put on the residual NPV (own default) and short a zero-strike call (counterparty default); $NPV(\tau_i)$ is the receiver-CDS cashflow value (premium annuity $\sum_i\alpha_iD(0,T_i)Q(\tau_1>T_i)$ less the accrual integral, plus $LGD_1\int D(0,t)dQ(\tau_1>t)$). Dependence: $\tau_i=\Lambda_i^{-1}(\xi_i)$, $U_i=1-e^{-\xi_i}$ linked by a Gaussian trivariate copula $C_R$; intensities $\lambda_j(t)=y_j(t)+\psi_j(t;\beta_j)$ with CIR $y_j$, $dy_j=\kappa_j(\mu_j-y_j)dt+\nu_j\sqrt{y_j}dZ_j$ (jumps set to zero). Model-to-market consistency uses the Monte Carlo shift $\Psi_i(t;\beta_i)=\log[P^{CIR}(0,t,\beta_i)/Q(\tau_i>t)^{\text{mkt}}]$ (Algorithm 1: draw $(U_0,U_1,U_2)$ from $C_R$, invert to $\tau_i$, accumulate discounted $LGD\cdot[\,\cdot\,]^+$).
- **Inputs:** per-name market CDS term structure (for hazard bootstrap), CIR parameters, copula correlation matrix $R$, LGDs, contract schedule.
- **Parameters (reported):** $LGD\in\{0.6,0.65,0.7\}$ low/middle/high; 5-year contract, quarterly payments; correlation triples such as $(-0.3,-0.3,0.6)$ and $(0.8,-0.3,-0.3)$; $\nu_1\in[0.01,0.50]$ (arXiv:0812.3705v4).
- **Observed effects:** payer-investor BR-CVA rises monotonically in $r_{12}$ while receiver-investor BR-CVA falls; CDS-implied vol rises 1.5%→42% as $\nu_1$ goes 0.01→0.50; the BA/Lehman/Shell mark-to-market runs 13.6–83.9 bp across scenarios; CIR calibration errors are below 1 bp, rising to 20 bp on a 355 bp BA quote and 23 bp on a 188 bp Lehman quote (arXiv:0812.3705v4).
- **Failure modes:** intensities are independent *processes* (only default times depend); no collateral, margin or netting; the Gaussian copula has no tail dependence; Monte Carlo error is non-trivial in low-$\nu$ rows; the adjustment is only meaningful if both parties include their own default (arXiv:0812.3705v4).

### 2.4 Jump-diffusion structural bond/CDS with Laplace inversion
**Purpose.** Price defaultable coupon bonds and CDS spreads when firm value can jump down (green-policy shocks), and recover the implied spread term structure.

$$\frac{dV_t}{V_t^-}=(r-\lambda\xi)dt+\sigma dW_t^Q+d\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N_t}(S_i-1)\Big),\qquad f_Y(y)=\eta e^{\eta y}\mathbf 1_{\{y<0\}},\qquad \xi=\frac{\eta}{\eta+1}-1,\qquad t_d=\inf\{t:V_t\le V_{def}\}$$
With $x=\ln V$, the default probability $D(x,t;T)=\mathbb E_t[\mathbf 1_{\{T\ge t_d\ge t\}}]$ has a closed-form Laplace transform $H(x,t;\omega)=\frac1\omega\mathbb E_t[\cdot]$ (Props. 1–2), from which the bond value (risk-free payment + recovery of face value $\Upsilon$ in default + coupon integral) and the CDS spread both follow:
$$\Pi(x,t;T)=\frac{(1-\Upsilon)\mathbb E[e^{-r(t_d-t)}\mathbf 1_{T\ge t_d\ge t}]}{\mathbb E[\int_t^Te^{-r(z-t)}\mathbf 1_{t_d>z}dz]}=\frac{\mathcal L^{-1}(F^{Prot}(x,t;\omega))}{\mathcal L^{-1}(F^{Prem}(x,t;\omega))}$$
with $F^{Prem},F^{Prot}$ built from $H(x,t;\omega+r)$ and inversion by the Gaver–Stehfest scheme with standard weights $\zeta_k$.
- **Inputs:** the CDS term structure (calibration target), equity value/volatility, barrier ratio $V_t/V_{def}$, recovery $\Upsilon$, plus carbon price and GHG data for the transition factor.
- **Parameters (reported):** $\Upsilon=0.6$; sensitivity at $V_t=4$, $V_{def}=1$, $b=0$, $r=0.02$, $\sigma=0.2$, $\lambda=0.4$, greenness $\eta\in\{5,5.5,6.5\}$; MAPE objective over 10 tenors; quantiles $\tau\in\{0.1,\dots,0.9\}$ (arXiv:2303.12483v1).
- **Observed effects:** higher $\eta$ (greener firm) ⇒ higher bond price and lower CDS spread, i.e. a positive green spread for brown firms; the jump model reproduces CDS dynamics with no significant calibration-error/transition-risk relation, while the no-jump model fails both (arXiv:2303.12483v1).
- **Failure modes:** the inverse Laplace is unstable — stability degrades as the summation order grows and round-off undermines the sum; constant $\lambda$, barrier and recovery; the carbon-price proxy "does not follow theoretical expectations" and was dropped; requiring complete CDS matrices and ratings is itself a selection effect (arXiv:2303.12483v1).

### 2.5 EE/PFE exposure profiles and a static-hedge network for Bermudan books
**Purpose.** Produce the exposure distributions feeding CVA and credit-limit monitoring for early-exercise products, including at risk horizons that do not coincide with exercise dates.

$$Q_{t_{m-1}}(S_{t_{m-1}})=B_{t_{m-1}}\mathbb E\Big[\frac{V_{t_m}(S_{t_m})}{B_{t_m}}\Big|S_{t_{m-1}}\Big],\quad V_{t_{m-1}}=\max\{h(S_{t_{m-1}}),Q_{t_{m-1}}(S_{t_{m-1}})\},\quad V_T=\max(h(S_T),0)$$
$$\tilde G_{\beta_t}(S_t)=W^\top\phi(S_t,b),\quad \phi_i(S_t,b_i)=\max\{i_{cp}(S_t-b_i),0\},\quad Q_{t_{m-1}}=\mathbb E[\tilde G_{\beta_{t_m}}(S_{t_m})|S_{t_{m-1}}]$$
$$EXP[t_m;S_{t_m}(\omega_j)]=V_{t_m}(S_{t_m}(\omega_j))\ \text{if}\ \tau(\omega_j)\ge t_m\ \text{else}\ 0,\qquad \tau=\min\{t_m:h(S_t)>Q_t(S_t)\},\qquad PFE_{99}(t_m)=q_{0.99}(EXP[t_m;\cdot])$$
- **Inputs:** simulated GBM paths, payoff $h$, strike, exercise grid, and either the risk-neutral or a real-world scenario set.
- **Parameters (reported):** constituent strikes in $[0.90,1.10]\times$spot; Adam $lr=0.001$, $\epsilon=10^{-8}$, bias floored at $10^{-8}$; stop when the loss stays below $10^{-8}$ for ten iterations; real-world $(\sigma_{real},\mu)\in\{(0.1,0.07),(0.3,0.1),(0.5,0.15),(0.5,0.01)\}$; PFE at 99% (arXiv:2402.15936v1).
- **Observed effects:** RLNN-OPT reaches the reference price in 3 epochs versus 25 for RLNN; EE and PFE align with COS across ATM/ITM/OTM and both measures and are "consistently better than the Longstaff method in all the cases considered"; intermediate-horizon exposures come from re-valuing the hedge portfolio with Black–Scholes, where direct LSM interpolation misaligns (cubic was the least bad LSM scheme) (arXiv:2402.15936v1).
- **Failure modes:** single-asset GBM only (constant $r,\sigma$); the network prices the instrument, so counterparty default, collateral and closeout are outside it; real-world scenarios vary only simulation, not pricing; convergence depends on the loss threshold and strike initialisation (arXiv:2402.15936v1).

### 2.6 Valuation architecture: bilateral CVA/DVA, closeout, netting, WWR and funding
**Purpose.** Fix the definitions before any number is computed, because the same portfolio admits materially different adjustments.
$$BVA=DVA-CVA,\quad BVA_{\text{bank}}=-BVA_{\text{corporate}},\quad \text{Price}=\text{risk-free price}+DVA-CVA,\quad CVA_{\text{netting}}\le CVA_{\text{no netting}}\ \text{(option on a sum)}$$
and $CVA\neq(\text{independence }CVA)\times(\text{fixed WWR multiplier})$. First-to-default killing: where the bank defaults first the DVA term activates and the CVA term vanishes; where the corporate defaults first the bank's DVA vanishes and its CVA activates. Contingent CDS payoff: on reference-credit default the seller pays $(1-\text{recovery})$ times the positive residual value of the reference portfolio — exactly the unilateral CVA the buyer measures.
- **Inputs:** trade-level positions and netting sets, CSA/margin terms, closeout convention (risk-free vs replacement), own and counterparty hazard rates, underlying $\mathbb Q$ dynamics, recovery.
- **Parameters (reported):** Credit VaR at the 99.9th percentile over a one-year horizon, simulated under $\mathbb P$ to the horizon and priced under $\mathbb Q$ thereafter; the collateral example uses recovery $0.4$ and a two-swap netting set of $+1$M / $-1$M USD (arXiv:1111.1331v3).
- **Observed effects:** the credit-quality-worsening term produced Citigroup's +$2.5bn CVA; Q3 2011 DVA gains were $1.9bn each at JPM and Citi, $1.7bn at BofA, $450m at Goldman; hedging DVA by *proxying* (selling protection on correlated peers) can invert, since a hedged peer's default makes the protection payment itself a stress — it can push the hedger toward default and so worsens systemic risk; closeout choice creates contagion in opposite directions (risk-free closeout harms the borrower under independence, replacement closeout further harms the lender under co-monotonicity) (arXiv:1111.1331v3).
- **Failure modes:** no unique CVA across institutions by construction ("payoff risk" from definitional choices, not only model risk); the additive `risk-free + DVA − CVA + FVA` form is not generally valid — consistent funding gives a recursive/BSDE problem; collateral is not protective against gap risk between margining dates (arXiv:1111.1331v3).

## 4. Pitfalls, validation and measurement

- **Calibration error is not model adequacy, and relative errors hide tenor-specific failure.** The transition-risk study finds the jump and no-jump models statistically indistinguishable in calibration-*error level* (t-test) yet only the jump model reproduces CDS dynamics; MAPE over ten tenors was chosen over RMSE to avoid over-weighting long-dated absolute errors, but one illiquid 30y point can still dominate — hence the 99th-percentile winsorisation and the requirement that no CDS tenor be missing or illiquid (arXiv:2303.12483v1, arXiv:0812.3705v4).
- **$\mathbb P$ vs $\mathbb Q$ confusion is the dominant leakage risk.** Credit VaR is simulated under $\mathbb P$ to the horizon then priced under $\mathbb Q$; CVA is a pure $\mathbb Q$ price. Fitting and evaluating on the same sample, or using realised default frequencies where a risk-neutral probability is required, silently biases every adjustment; the FAQ's cure is joint Kalman/non-linear filtering over price history and risk-neutral expectations together (arXiv:1111.1331v3).
- **Inputs, not models, are the binding constraint.** Where no liquid CDS or bond exists, $\mathbb Q$ default probabilities, recoveries, spread volatilities and credit-underlying correlations are proxied from $\mathbb P$ statistics or ratings — an approximation the FAQ states explicitly. A credit module must expose parameter provenance, not just values (arXiv:1111.1331v3).
- **Model risk is structural, not incidental.** CVA on a swap book needs an interest-rate option model, so a previously model-independent valuation becomes model-dependent, and CVA values differ materially across institutions by payoff definition alone: unilateral vs bilateral, closeout convention, collateral and re-hypothecation, first-to-default treatment, funding (arXiv:1111.1331v3).
- **Wrong-way risk is not a multiplier.** Compute-CVA-under-independence-then-multiply fails because the multiplier varies strongly with dynamics, volatilities and correlations and is very volatile across asset classes — unsafe, especially for systemic scenarios. Model WWR explicitly through the dependence between default and the underlying, as in the copula-intensity construction (arXiv:1111.1331v3, arXiv:0812.3705v4).
- **Dependence assumptions are unvalidated by construction.** Gaussian copulas have no tail dependence and are attacked as static and time-unstable; the structural-contagion model answers with an explicit causal channel but then produces the equally unrealistic instant cross-default. Validate any copula fit against at least one path-dependent alternative (the order-of-defaults recursion of arXiv:2509.02347v2) before trusting basket numbers (arXiv:0710.0753v1, arXiv:0812.3705v4).
- **Numerical error can exceed model error, so the validation protocol matters.** Inverse Laplace transforms degrade with summation order, sparse-grid quadrature is needed for the 2-D survival integrals, and Monte Carlo standard errors in the CVA tables are large enough at low spread volatility to reorder results — report standard errors beside every adjustment. The protocol implied by the corpus: (i) recover analytic survival/price formulas by Monte Carlo on the same parameters (the $n^{\text{th}}$-CDS paper checks 10,000 paths and an independent reflection-principle solution); (ii) test that calibration errors carry *no* significant relation to the risk factor the model claims to capture, via panel quantile regression over $\tau\in\{0.1,\dots,0.9\}$ with firm fixed effects and bootstrapped standard errors; (iii) check the full 0.5–30y term structure, not one tenor; (iv) benchmark exposure profiles against an independent pricer (COS) at every horizon and against interpolation baselines at intermediate horizons; (v) perturb the barrier slope in equity-calibrated structural models and confirm spread stability (arXiv:2509.02347v2, arXiv:2303.12483v1, arXiv:2402.15936v1, arXiv:0710.0753v1).
- **In-sample traps specific to credit.** Bootstrapping piecewise-linear hazard rates and re-using them to compute the shift $\Psi$ is fit-then-evaluate; the bilateral-CVA paper does this deliberately for illustration and reports the residual 1–23 bp errors, which is the honest way to use it. Likewise a quantile regression whose dependent variable is the model's *own* implied spread change always looks significant — regress it on an exogenous factor instead and require the error term to be insignificant (arXiv:0812.3705v4, arXiv:2303.12483v1).

## 5. Recommended first implementation in FinRobot

1. **(S) Firm-value / distance-to-default proxy.** Per-name structural inputs: equity OHLCV plus market cap and debt from fundamentals, mapped to $V_0$, $\sigma_E$ and a barrier ratio, with asset volatility from iterating the Merton equity-to-asset relation. Data: `core_stock_apis` (eodhd) + `fundamental_data` (fmp) + `technical_indicators` (alpha_vantage) + `risk_free_curve`. Value: unblocks every structural model in §2.1/§2.4 from data FinRobot already serves (arXiv:0710.0753v1, arXiv:2303.12483v1). Risk: debt is a single scalar, not a maturity ladder, and no CDS exists to check the implied spread.
2. **(S) Two-name first-passage CDS/bond engine.** Implement §2.1 verbatim — exponential barriers, the double-Bessel joint survival series, first/second-to-default spreads, the bond-yield identity. Data: item-1 inputs plus `risk_free_curve`. Value: the only fully analytic credit-spread generator available without market credit data, and it exercises contagion and correlation directly (arXiv:0710.0753v1). Risk: quadrature and series truncation must be validated against Monte Carlo first.
3. **(M) Jump-diffusion CDS term-structure engine with Laplace inversion.** Implement §2.4: downward-jump firm value, constant barrier, Laplace transforms for bond and CDS legs, Gaver–Stehfest inversion, and the $\eta$ sensitivity curves ($r=0.02$, $\sigma=0.2$, $\lambda=0.4$, $\Upsilon=0.6$). Data: item-1 inputs; no market CDS needed for sensitivity work. Value: reproduces the corpus's clearest evidence that jumps, not diffusion, generate the short-end spread, and gives a concentration-risk scenario tool (arXiv:2303.12483v1). Risk: $\lambda$, $\eta$ and the barrier are not identifiable against a gap feed — expose them as scenario ranges and guard the inversion against round-off.
4. **(M) Bilateral CVA engine on the served SPX option book.** Implement §2.3: Gaussian-copula triggers over CIR intensities, contract-level valuation, and the long-put/short-call adjustment with the four default-ordering events. Data: `options_surface` (cboe) plus `options_data` (moomoo gateway) for the book, `risk_free_curve` for discounting. Value: the symmetric adjustment both sides agree on, directly reproducing the corpus' central result and its correlation/volatility sensitivity (arXiv:0812.3705v4). Risk: with no counterparty PDs the default parameters are scenario inputs; only the portfolio and rate sides are data-grounded today.
5. **(M) NumPy EE/PFE exposure profiler for a Bermudan book.** Implement §2.5's static-hedge representation and exposure definition, producing EE and 99% PFE at exercise dates and intermediate horizons. Data: an option chain (`options_surface`/`options_data`) for the reference pricer and `core_stock_apis` for paths. Value: the exposure distribution that feeds CVA and limit monitoring at zero extra cost over pricing, and it stays inside the pure-NumPy dependency line (three layers; weights from an OLS step and only the strikes move under Adam, so no `torch` is needed) (arXiv:2402.15936v1). Risk: GBM-only, single underlier, and it models the instrument rather than the counterparty, so pair it with item 4.
6. **(S) CVA definition and validation harness.** Fix the definitional choices up front (unilateral/bilateral, closeout convention, netting, collateral, first-to-default), pin the netting inequality as a unit test, and add the corpus' validation battery: Monte-Carlo recovery of analytic survival functions, full-tenor term-structure reproduction, calibration-error/risk-factor independence tests, and Monte-Carlo standard errors beside every adjustment. Data: none beyond items 1–5. Value: prevents the payoff risk and metric-shopping the FAQ documents as the main source of inter-institution dispersion (arXiv:1111.1331v3, arXiv:2509.02347v2, arXiv:2303.12483v1).
7. **(L) Contingent CDS and multi-name basket module.** The $n^{\text{th}}$-to-default recursion, the CCDS payoff as the exact hedge of unilateral CVA, and multi-name contagion are implementable, but they multiply the reference-entity count and therefore the default-parameter surface that is already the binding gap. Data: none new. Value: a genuine hedge instrument for the adjustment computed in item 4 (arXiv:2509.02347v2, arXiv:1111.1331v3). Risk: without an issuer-level credit feed, correlations and intensities stay scenario inputs, so the module can be stress-tested but not calibrated, and the path combinatorics grow fast in $N$.
8. **(—) Out of scope until a credit feed exists.** Issuer-level CDS/bond calibration, tranche-implied copula parameters, credit-spread volatility, transition-risk factors (carbon price, GHG) and CVA on IRS books are blocked on §3 gap rows, not on modelling difficulty. State this rather than approximating them from equity proxies without labelling the approximation (arXiv:0812.3705v4, arXiv:2303.12483v1, arXiv:1111.1331v3).
